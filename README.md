# Financial Responsibility for Advanced AI Systems

**Second place, MIT AI Alignment (MAIA) Technical Governance Competition · Featured in Forbes**

A policy proposal by **Sridharan and Bhardwaj** to pair AI liability with the financial capacity to compensate people harmed by AI systems. Developed for the MAIA competition, the proposal adds a financial-responsibility framework to the AI LEAD Act through a proposed Title VI.

| Document | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| [Policy proposal](documents/Title-VI_Policy-Artifact_Sridharan-Bhardwaj.pdf) | Two-page legislative text covering tiered financial responsibility, a compensation fund, insurance coverage, and a conditional safe harbor. |
| [Supporting report](documents/Title-VI_Supporting-Report_Sridharan-Bhardwaj.pdf) | Fourteen-page explanation of the proposal, its rationale, implementation choices, limitations, and interview notes. |

The proposal addresses a practical gap: establishing liability does not ensure that an injured person can collect compensation. Its main mechanisms are:

- **Tiered financial responsibility:** insurance, surety bonds, letters of credit, or qualifying self-insurance, with obligations triggered by training compute or covered-product revenue.
- **An Advanced AI Compensation Fund:** pooled contributions from the highest-tier developers and a federal backstop for qualifying large incidents.
- **Explicit insurance coverage:** a requirement for insurers to affirm or exclude AI-related coverage, with a default toward coverage when a policy is silent.
- **A conditional safe harbor:** a rebuttable presumption of reasonable care for specified claims when developers demonstrate substantial compliance with designated risk-management standards.

These documents present a proposed legislative addendum, not enacted law. The PDFs are preserved as supplied, including the supporting report's interview notes.
